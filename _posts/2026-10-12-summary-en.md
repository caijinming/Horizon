### [Terence Tao&\#x27;s &\#x27;Math 2.0&\#x27; slides on AI&\#x27;s role in mathematical research](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/slides/math-2-0-caltech-2026.pdf) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Terence Tao, one of the world&\#x27;s most prominent mathematicians, has made the slides from his talk &quot;Math 2.0&quot; — filed as a 2026 Caltech presentation — freely available as a PDF, arguing that AI is changing how mathematical research is practiced rather than announcing a new tool or verified result. Slides cited in the ensuing Hacker News thread include one questioning whether patients would accept an AI-derived cancer cure that no human researcher fully understands, and the framing that &quot;open problems are lighthouses not destinations.&quot; The posting drew 323 points and 372 comments, several of which relayed secondhand reports that a recent OpenAI release included solutions to problems mathematicians had worked on for years. As a slide deck, it is a position statement from a notable practitioner, not a shipped capability or independently measured result.

hackernews · Anon84 · Oct 11, 13:16 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50043026)

**「The OpenAI proof flood」** Tao&\#x27;s presentation follows OpenAI&\#x27;s October 6 release of a large batch of mathematics manuscripts — described as nearly 400 proofs spanning 372 result families across pure mathematics and theoretical computer science — which Tao suggested had been released irresponsibly and risked making mathematics &quot;less fertile&quot;. Public commentary on the deck, including mathematician Samuel Allen Alexander&\#x27;s video walkthrough, explicitly frames Math 2.0 as Tao&\#x27;s response to that flood of AI-generated proofs.

**「Practical impact on researchers」** For working mathematicians, the disruption Tao describes is already operational rather than speculative: on October 6, 2026, OpenAI released more than 700 manuscripts at once claiming solutions to hundreds of open math problems, and the math blog Proofs and Prompts collected over 100 researcher responses to the release. The immediate consequence is a verification and planning burden: researchers, journals, and funders must check AI-generated proofs before building on them and reassess research programs organized around now-claimed problems, since observers noted the results were uneven in significance even as Clay Mathematics Institute president Martin Bridson called the release breathtaking.

**「Community discussion」** The sharpest pushback came from drug-discovery and medical commenters who called Tao&\#x27;s cancer-medicine slide out of his depth, arguing that many treatments — general anesthesia among them — already work without fully understood mechanisms, so demanding a human who understands the cure is not how medicine operates. Others engaged his broader thesis seriously: one commenter glossed &quot;open problems are lighthouses&quot; as pure mathematics maintaining a shared &quot;codebase&quot; of theory and definitions that current math AI does not extend elegantly, and another described researchers reacting with grief to reports that an OpenAI release had closed out problems they had worked on for years.

### [&\#x27;Passkeys are a trap&\#x27; essay draws technical pushback on Hacker News](https://nonfunctional.substack.com/p/passkeys-are-a-trap-do-not-use-them) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Substack essay titled &\#x27;Passkeys are a trap&\#x27; argues readers should not adopt passkeys, building its case on remote attestation — a WebAuthn feature that can let websites verify and restrict which authenticators may register credentials — and on platform/vendor lock-in. It is an opinion piece rather than a report of a newly discovered flaw or shipped change, and even its own closing addendum concedes that passkeys are, in most cases, domain-bound and therefore resistant to man-in-the-middle attacks. The post drew a large technical debate on Hacker News \(281 points, 155 comments\), where discussion turned on specifics such as when attestation can actually be enforced and how passkey portability compares with password managers.

hackernews · laurex · Oct 11, 21:59 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50048312)

**「Passkeys and remote attestation, briefly」** Passkeys are WebAuthn credentials that replace passwords by keeping a private key on a user&\#x27;s device or in a platform vendor&\#x27;s synced account \(such as Apple&\#x27;s or Google&\#x27;s\), with websites verifying signatures instead of shared secrets. Remote attestation is an optional part of the same standard in which the authenticator cryptographically identifies its manufacturer and model at credential registration, letting a server confirm the key was created by a specific vendor&\#x27;s implementation rather than a third-party password manager or hardware token. The article&\#x27;s critique rests on how that mechanism, combined with syncing that is controlled by platform vendors, could let websites dictate which authenticators users are allowed to use.

**「Practical trade-offs for passkey adopters」** For organizations and users adopting passkeys, the immediate consequence is a portability-versus-control trade-off: synced passkeys can move between a user&\#x27;s devices within one ecosystem such as Apple&\#x27;s iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager, while device-bound passkeys offer stronger enterprise-grade management but tie the credential to a single device. A compatibility concern raised in the discussion is that remote attestation — the mechanism the post warns could enable lock-in — can only be enforced when a credential is first registered, and browsers show a permission prompt when a site requests it, so sites cannot retroactively require attestation from already-registered credentials. Users worried about vendor lock-in do have a concrete avenue to watch: the FIDO Alliance publishes credential exchange specifications aimed at enabling passkeys to move between providers.

**「Community debate」** Commenters challenged the essay&\#x27;s threat model: agwa noted that attestation can only be required during credential registration — with a browser permission prompt — and cannot be enforced retroactively at login, saying he doubted major sites like Netflix or Twitter actually reject unattested registrations. bmitch3020 described voluntarily avoiding passkeys over portability and control, preferring backed-up password-manager credentials to device-bound biometric unlock, while MathMonkeyMan and danpalmer pointed out that the essay&\#x27;s own text concedes legitimate attestation uses and passkeys&\#x27; man-in-the-middle resistance.

### [China Seeks Public Comment on Food Safety Law Overhaul Covering Live-Stream Sales and School Meals](https://weibo.com/1642634100/Rm8MOi0jg) ⭐️ 8.0/10

China&\#x27;s market regulator has opened public comment on a draft revision of the Food Safety Law — its first large-scale systematic overhaul since the law took effect in 2009 — that would for the first time bring live-streamed food sales under the law, regulating platform operators, in-platform sellers, and livestream room operators. The draft also adds a dedicated school food safety section establishing a principal \(or kindergarten head\) responsibility system and mandatory meal-accompaniment rules for primary and secondary schools and kindergartens; the measures remain a draft for comment, not final law.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 11, 06:02

**「Background」** China&\#x27;s Food Safety Law has been in force since 2009, before livestream selling — hosts promoting and selling goods to viewers in real time — became widespread, while the &quot;meal accompaniment&quot; system means designated school leaders eat the same meals as students to oversee their safety. The market regulator had already opened a separate public consultation in March 2026 focused on tightening kindergarten food-safety responsibilities.

**「Impact」** If adopted, the draft would impose new compliance obligations on online food trading platforms, livestream sellers, and other food businesses, while making principals personally responsible for meal safety at primary and secondary schools and kindergartens.

**「Impact」** Korean equity investors are most exposed because the market&\#x27;s heavy reliance on Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix channels any shift in AI sentiment directly into concentrated selling pressure, with turnover already down about 70% from its May peak.
